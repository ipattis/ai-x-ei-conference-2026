Context

What does "edge AI" actually mean?

Cloud AI — what you're used to

Your prompt travels to a data centre (OpenAI, Google, Microsoft). A huge model runs on GPU clusters costing millions. A response comes back. Your data touched someone else's system.

Edge AI — what's now possible

The model is the browser tab. Inference runs on your own hardware — CPU or GPU — using WebAssembly or WebGPU. Nothing leaves the device. Ever.

Why now?

  • 1Quantisation — 4-bit models are 4–8× smaller with minimal quality loss
  • 2WebGPU — browser-native GPU access, zero install required
  • 3Open weights — Gemma, Qwen, Llama, Phi: free to download and run
  • 4Transformers.js — HuggingFace brings the full ML pipeline to JavaScript
  • 5Device capability — modern school laptops have integrated GPUs that handle inference

Pillar 2 — System Prompting

You define the AI's identity

A system prompt is the instruction that runs before any student interaction. It shapes everything: tone, vocabulary level, what topics to avoid, what role to play.

Year 6 Reading Companion

"You are Archie, a friendly reading buddy for Year 6 pupils aged 10–11. Always use simple vocabulary. Never give away plot answers — ask the pupil what THEY think happens next. Keep responses under 4 sentences."

Teacher CPD Planner

"You are a coaching assistant for teachers. Ask clarifying questions before making suggestions. Ground all advice in the DfE Teachers' Standards. Avoid edu-jargon."

What a system prompt controls

  • 🎭Persona — name, role, personality
  • 📏Reading level — GCSE, KS2, adult
  • 🚫Topic boundaries — what to refuse or redirect
  • 💬Response style — Socratic, direct, encouraging
  • 🌍Values — your school's ethos, safeguarding tone
  • 📝Format — bullet points, short answers, markdown
A teacher who knows their learners writes better system prompts than any product manager at a tech company ever will.
Pillar 3 — Knowledge Base / RAG

The AI knows what you teach it

RAG — Retrieval Augmented Generation — means the AI searches your uploaded documents before answering, then grounds its response in what it finds.

How it works — locally, in the browser

  1. 1Upload a .txt document — curriculum spec, policy, marking guide
  2. 2Split into chunks (~500 chars each) + dense vector embeddings created
  3. 3Stored in IndexedDB — on-device, survives page reloads
  4. 4Pupil asks a question → relevant chunks retrieved
  5. 5Chunks injected into model context before it replies

What to upload

AQA Spec School Marking Policy Reading List SEN Guidelines Lesson Plans School Handbook CPD Notes Safeguarding Policy

Hybrid scoring — smarter retrieval

Combines semantic similarity (meaning) with keyword matching (exact terms) — 70/30 blend. "What does the spec say about quadratics?" finds the right chunk even without the word "spec".

All vectors in browser IndexedDB. No cloud. No third party. No data-protection headache.

// Hybrid: 70% semantic + 30% keyword const scored = allChunks.map(chunk => ({ ...chunk, score: 0.7 * cosineSimilarity(queryEmb, chunk.emb) + 0.3 * keywordScore(query, chunk.text) })); const context = scored .filter(c => c.score >= 0.15) .sort((a, b) => b.score - a.score) .slice(0, 3000 /* chars */);

Safeguarding & Data Protection

What happens to student data?

With this approach

  • ✅All inference on-device — zero network requests during use
  • ✅Conversation history in browser's IndexedDB — local only
  • ✅Knowledge base stored locally as embeddings
  • ✅No account required — no identity linkage
  • ✅GDPR-friendly by design — no processor agreement needed
  • ✅Works on an air-gapped school network

What Big Tech tools require

  • ⚠️Student data processed on third-party servers
  • ⚠️Data retention policies vary — and change without notice
  • ⚠️US-based cloud = Schrems II / international transfer risk
  • ⚠️Age-verification obligations for under-13s
  • ⚠️DPA / data processor agreement required
"The most compliant AI tool is one where the data never leaves the building."
