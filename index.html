Reclaiming
the Narrative

AI in education has largely been shaped by commercial interests. This session is about what becomes possible when schools decide to shape it themselves.

Before we go any further

This isn't coming.
It's already here.

In early 2020, a novel virus entered the world. Within weeks it was everywhere. There was no reversing it — only responding to it. Society had to adapt, and adapt fast.

AI is the same kind of event. Not a product cycle. Not a trend. A threshold — and we crossed it. The question is no longer whether to engage with it. It is how, and on whose terms.

Disengagement is not neutrality. It is just a different kind of choice.

We have been here before.

When writing emerged in the ancient world, it didn't just record knowledge — it restructured how knowledge was held, shared, and trusted. Socrates himself was a sceptic. He worried that writing would weaken memory, that wisdom transmitted through text would be hollow — mere appearance of understanding without the substance.

He wasn't entirely wrong. Something was lost. But what came after — philosophy, science, literature, law — was worth building.

Our generation is tasked with making this paradigm shift land well.
A recent headline worth sitting with

"The Death of Khanmigo"

Khan Academy launched Khanmigo in 2023 with genuine ambition: an AI tutor for every student, available around the clock, personalised and patient.

It was built on GPT-4. It cost $4 per month per student. It was backed by a Gates Foundation grant. The vision was real. The rollout was real.

And then, quietly, it didn't stick.

Why it didn't land

  • 🔗Dependent on OpenAI's servers — latency, outages, cost
  • 🎭Generic persona. No relationship to the teacher, the school, the class
  • 💳Subscription friction in under-resourced settings
  • 🧱No way for educators to shape what it says or how it behaves
The tool was impressive. The fit was missing.
Why this moment is different — the arc of AI products

Intelligence has been decentralising
since the beginning.

  • 1Chat to a server — you type, a remote model responds. ChatGPT, early Claude. Intelligence lives in a data centre.
  • 2Chat to a server with tools — the model can browse the web, write code, call APIs. More powerful, still centralised.
  • 3Multiple servers working together — agent frameworks, multi-model pipelines. Powerful, complex, expensive.
  • 4Smaller, optimised models — Gemma, Phi, Mistral. Efficient enough to run on powerful consumer hardware. The cloud is no longer mandatory.
  • 5Models in the browser itself — WebGPU, Transformers.js. No server. No account. No subscription. The model runs where you are.
The trend is clear and it's continuing. I think educators should care about where it leads.
A confession from someone who works in AI

I actively avoid
algorithms in my personal life.

My kids will tell you my favourite radio station is SCAN — I go station to station looking for a good local DJ, and when I find one I stay with them. The curation is human. The serendipity is real.

That instinct isn't anti-technology. It's pro-relationship. And I think educators understand that distinction better than anyone.

An honest look at how we got here

We've been given tools
and asked to adapt to them.

The companies that built AI infrastructure also wrote the story of what "AI in education" means. That's not a conspiracy. It's just how markets work.

But the story they've written tends to have the same shape: a subscription, a dashboard, a chatbot built for everyone and therefore shaped by no one in particular — certainly not by the teacher in front of 28 Year 9s on a Friday afternoon.

The default EdTech narrative

  • 💳Here is the product. Here is the price per seat.
  • 🔒Your students' interactions inform our model.
  • 📋The features are what we decide the features are.
  • 🔧Adapt your practice to the tool, not the other way around.
Most schools have become consumers of a narrative they didn't author.

The shift in power this creates

When the model lives on your device,
everything changes.

You decide what it knows. You decide how it speaks. You decide what it values, whose interests it serves, what it refuses to engage with.

You are no longer adapting to someone else's tool. You are building something that belongs to your school, reflects your pedagogy, and serves your specific learners.

The most important word in "AI in education" has always been the one in the middle.

Three things you now have the power to do

  • 1 Define its character — write the personality, values, and constraints yourself. A sentence or two. No code required.
  • 2 Ground it in your knowledge — give it your curriculum spec, your marking policy, your SEN guidelines. It reads your documents, not the internet.
  • 3 Own the data completely — nothing leaves the device. No cloud processing, no third-party servers, no data protection headaches.
What it looks like in practice — character

A teacher who knows their learners
is the best prompt engineer in the room.

Before the AI speaks to a single student, you give it its instructions. Its name. Its personality. What it should encourage. What it should redirect. The reading level it should use. The values it should embody.

If you can write a brief for a teaching assistant, you can do this.

A Year 6 reading companion

"You are Archie, a friendly reading buddy for Year 6 pupils aged 10–11. Always use simple vocabulary. Never give away plot answers — ask the pupil what THEY think happens next. Keep responses under 4 sentences."

A Sixth Form debate coach

"You always argue the opposing side. Push back with evidence and questions. Never validate a weak argument — make the student work harder to defend it."

A teacher's CPD thinking partner

"You are a reflective coaching assistant. Ask one open question at a time. Ground all suggestions in the DfE Teachers' Standards. Avoid jargon."

These are the complete instructions. That's all it takes.

The question every leader should ask

When you deploy any AI tool,
where does the trust actually sit?

With cloud-based tools, you are trusting a third party with your students' data, their conversations, their learning struggles. You are trusting that their data policies won't change. That their servers won't be breached. That their terms of service stay favourable.

That trust is not unreasonable — but it is a risk you are choosing to carry, often without fully examining it.

"The most compliant AI tool is one where the data never leaves the building."

With a device-local approach

  • ✅All processing happens on the device — zero data in transit
  • ✅No account means no identity linked to any conversation
  • ✅GDPR-friendly by architecture, not just by policy
  • ✅Works on an air-gapped network — no internet required after setup
  • ✅No data processor agreement needed — there is no processor

This isn't a compliance workaround. It's a fundamentally different risk profile — one where the school holds all the cards.

Returning to where we started

If AI handles the what and the how —
what does that free us to be?

The answer isn't fewer teachers. It's more human teaching.

The empathy in the room when a student is struggling. The creative leap that changes how a concept clicks. The character modelled in how a teacher handles difficulty. The sense of purpose that makes a young person believe their learning matters.

Those are not things AI can provide. They are what we protect — by refusing to outsource the meaning of education to tools that don't understand it.

The intelligence is a commodity now. The pedagogy never will be.

The best AI in education won't replace the human relationship. It'll protect the space for it.

Appendix B — How the technology works

How the technology actually works

Cloud AI — what you're used to

Your prompt travels to a data centre. A huge model runs on GPU clusters. A response comes back. Your data touched someone else's system.

Local AI — what's now possible

The model runs in the browser itself, on your device's GPU, using an open standard called WebGPU. Nothing leaves the device. Ever.

Why this is possible now

  • 1Quantisation — models compressed to 4-bit are 4–8× smaller with minimal quality loss
  • 2WebGPU — browser-native GPU access, no installation required
  • 3Open weights — Google's Gemma 4 is free to download and run (Apache 2.0)
  • 4Transformers.js — HuggingFace's library brings the full inference pipeline to JavaScript
  • 5Device capability — modern laptops have integrated GPUs that handle this comfortably
Appendix C — Defining the AI's character

System prompting: the full picture

A system prompt is a hidden instruction the AI receives before any user interaction. It shapes every response the model gives — permanently, for the life of the session.

What it can control

  • 🎭Persona — name, role, personality
  • 📏Reading level — GCSE, KS2, adult professional
  • 🚫Boundaries — what to refuse or redirect
  • 💬Pedagogy — Socratic, direct, scaffolded
  • 🌍Values — your school's ethos, safeguarding tone
  • 📝Format — response length, structure, language

A Socratic maths tutor

"You are a patient maths tutor for GCSE students. Never give the answer. When a student is stuck, ask one guiding question that helps them reach the next step themselves. Use plain language."

An EAL vocabulary companion

"You help students learning English as an additional language. When introducing new words, always give a simple definition and one example sentence. Be patient and warm. Never use idioms without explaining them."
Appendix D — Grounding it in your knowledge

How the knowledge base works

This technique is called Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG). The AI searches your documents before every reply and injects the most relevant passages into its context.

The process, step by step

  1. 1You upload a plain text document
  2. 2It's split into ~500-character chunks with overlap
  3. 3Each chunk is converted to a mathematical representation (embedding) and stored locally
  4. 4On each query, the most relevant chunks are retrieved
  5. 5Those chunks are added to the model's context before it responds

Hybrid retrieval — why it works

Combines semantic similarity (what the question means) with keyword matching (exact terms) — a 70/30 blend. This means "what does the spec say about quadratics?" finds the right passage even if the word "spec" doesn't appear in it.

Privacy

All embeddings are stored in the browser's local database (IndexedDB). Nothing is sent to any server. The documents themselves are never transmitted. Clear the browser data and it's gone.

RAG — retrieve relevant chunks

// Hybrid: 70% semantic + 30% keyword const scored = allChunks.map(chunk => ({ ...chunk, score: 0.7 * cosineSimilarity(queryEmb, chunk.emb) + 0.3 * keywordScore(query, chunk.text) })); const context = scored .filter(c => c.score >= 0.15) .sort((a, b) => b.score - a.score) .slice(0, 3000);
